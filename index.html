Loading the emulator…

What this is. A JavaScript port of METEOR's generation kernel, driven by a compact bundle exported from a model trained offline on CMIP6 output. The whole payload is about 300 KB and there is no server: the emulator runs on your machine.

Two generations of scenario. The CMIP6 SSPs and the CMIP7 ScenarioMIP markers were built years apart and harmonized to different vintages of history, so they are not interchangeable. Both are driven here through the same simple climate model, CICERO-SCM, which is what makes comparing them meaningful at all: taking CMIP7's forcing from its own MAGICC assessment instead would have put a 0.74 W/m² model difference inside what looks like a scenario difference.

Baselines. Temperature, and the maps, are shown as change from a reference period: 1850–1900, the pre-industrial convention warming levels are defined against, or 2005–2024, recent history. Either is the model's own forced response averaged over the period under CMIP7 Medium, and every scenario shares it, so the difference between two scenarios does not depend on the choice. Measuring from recent history removes the part of the spread between models that each inherited from its own historical warming — the models here warmed by between 0.7 and 1.6 °C from 1850–1900 to 2005–2024 — so switching baselines is a quick way to see how much of their disagreement about the future is disagreement about the past. The precipitation timeseries stays in absolute mm/day.

What it is not. One model at a time, so the spread you see is that model's internal variability — not model uncertainty, and not the full uncertainty in a projection. Switching models shows how much they disagree; the band around any one of them does not. Maps show the forced response only; variability at a region you draw yourself would need a further 11 MB of EOF maps that this page does not load. Custom emissions need a simple climate model, which is not here either, so the scenarios on offer are the ones the bundle was built with. Precipitation covers 2015–2100 only, the window its distribution transform was fitted for.
